# CASE FILE 1014-HM: The Death of Edmund Harrow

**Hollowmere Manor, Saturday 14 October. Prepared by Inspector Dana Whitlock, Ashford Constabulary.**

---

## 1. Case Summary
[doc_type: summary | case: 1014-HM]

Edmund Harrow, 64, a wealthy art collector and owner of Harrow & Finch Galleries, was found dead in his study at Hollowmere Manor at approximately 9:40 PM on Saturday 14 October. He was discovered slumped in his armchair by his wife, Margaret Harrow. The cause of death was cardiac arrest brought on by a lethal dose of digitoxin, a poison derived from foxglove (Digitalis purpurea). Six people were in the house that evening during a dinner party celebrating the gallery's 25th anniversary. The study door was closed but not locked. No signs of forced entry were found on any door or window.

## 2. The Victim: Edmund Harrow
[doc_type: profile | person: Edmund Harrow | role: victim]

Edmund Harrow, age 64, built Harrow & Finch Galleries from a single shop into a firm with three locations. He was known as exacting and private. He drank one glass of brandy from the study decanter every evening at about 9:00 PM, a habit known to everyone in the household and to most regular guests. In the week before his death he had told Margaret he was "settling an important matter about the gallery's inventory." He had commissioned an independent appraisal of the gallery's flagship collection in September. He had no history of heart disease and passed his annual physical in June.

## 3. Scene Report: The Study
[doc_type: scene_report | location: study | author: Inspector Whitlock]

The study is on the ground floor, east wing, reached by a corridor from the main hall. The body was in a leather armchair beside the desk. A crystal brandy glass lay on its side on the carpet; about a centimetre of liquid remained. The decanter on the side table was two-thirds full. The desk lamp was on. In the fireplace, which had not been lit, officers recovered the charred and torn remains of a typed letter (see Evidence E3). A gold cufflink engraved "O.F." was found under the desk by Constable Reyes (Evidence E5). The window was latched from inside. The room smelled faintly of brandy and, according to Constable Reyes, "something green, like cut stems."

## 4. Timeline of the Evening
[doc_type: timeline | date: 14 October]

- **6:30 PM** – Guests arrive. Drinks in the drawing room.
- **7:30 PM** – Dinner served in the dining room.
- **8:15 PM** – Julian Harrow and his father argue on the terrace (heard by Thomas Greaves, the butler).
- **8:30 PM** – Greaves tops up the study decanter from a fresh sealed bottle, as he does each evening.
- **8:45 PM** – Dinner ends. Guests move to the drawing room.
- **8:50 PM** – Oliver Finch leaves the drawing room, saying he must take a phone call.
- **8:58 PM** – Greaves sees a man in a grey jacket in the east corridor, heading away from the study.
- **9:00 PM** – Edmund excuses himself to the study for his nightly brandy. Julian says he went to the billiard room alone.
- **9:10 PM** – Dr. Priya Nair and Clara Voss are seen together by Margaret in the conservatory.
- **9:15 PM** – Finch returns to the drawing room.
- **9:40 PM** – Margaret finds Edmund dead and screams. Greaves calls emergency services at 9:43 PM.
- **10:05 PM** – Paramedics pronounce death. Police arrive 10:20 PM.

## 5. Suspect Profile: Margaret Harrow
[doc_type: profile | person: Margaret Harrow | role: wife | age: 58]

Edmund's wife of 31 years. She stands to inherit the manor and half of the gallery's value under the current will. Staff describe the marriage as "cool but loyal." She says she spent the evening with guests in the drawing room until she went to check on Edmund at 9:40. She is the first person recorded entering the study after Edmund. She reports nothing unusual about the room except that "the window was shut tight, which was odd, Edmund always liked it cracked open."

## 6. Suspect Profile: Julian Harrow
[doc_type: profile | person: Julian Harrow | role: son | age: 31]

Edmund's only son. Works nominally at the gallery but has accumulated gambling debts of roughly £48,000, according to bank records. Edmund had refused to cover them. Julian was heard arguing with his father on the terrace at 8:15 PM. He wears a navy blazer. His shoe size is 11. He says he spent 9:00 to 9:45 alone in the billiard room, and has no one to confirm this.

## 7. Suspect Profile: Dr. Priya Nair
[doc_type: profile | person: Priya Nair | role: family physician | age: 45]

The Harrow family doctor for twelve years and a dinner guest. As a physician she has access to cardiac medication, including digoxin, a close chemical relative of digitoxin. Edmund had recently raised concerns about billing irregularities at her clinic, which she says were "a clerical misunderstanding." Her medical bag stayed in the main hall all evening; an inventory shows every vial accounted for. She wore a dark green dress. She says she was in the conservatory with Clara Voss from about 9:05 PM.

## 8. Suspect Profile: Clara Voss
[doc_type: profile | person: Clara Voss | role: art appraiser | age: 39]

An independent appraiser hired by Edmund in September to authenticate the gallery's flagship collection. She completed her report on 10 October and sent it to Edmund only. She was invited to the party as a courtesy. She wore a cream suit. She states she was in the conservatory with Dr. Nair from 9:05 PM and that the two spoke about orchids. She declines to describe her report's findings, citing client confidentiality, though she admits the report "would have upset several people."

## 9. Suspect Profile: Oliver Finch
[doc_type: profile | person: Oliver Finch | role: business partner | age: 60]

Edmund's partner and co-owner of Harrow & Finch Galleries for 25 years. Handles the financial and inventory side of the business. He wore a grey jacket and gold cufflinks. His shoe size is 10. He says he stepped outside to the garden at 8:50 PM to take a call from a buyer in Zurich, and returned at about 9:15 PM. He says he lost one cufflink "weeks ago, probably during the September board meeting at the manor." He describes Edmund as "my oldest friend."

## 10. Suspect Profile: Thomas Greaves
[doc_type: profile | person: Thomas Greaves | role: butler | age: 60]

Butler at Hollowmere for 22 years. He handled the decanter and the evening service. He has no known financial motive; Edmund's will leaves him a modest bequest of £10,000. His shoe size is 9. He wore the standard house livery (black). He is considered by the household as unfailingly reliable.

## 11. Witness Statement: Thomas Greaves
[doc_type: witness_statement | person: Thomas Greaves | time: 8:15-9:43 PM]

"At about quarter past eight I was on the terrace clearing glasses when Mr. Julian and his father began shouting. I heard Mr. Julian say, 'You owe me this much,' and Mr. Edmund replied that he'd 'not another penny.' At half past eight I filled the study decanter from a new sealed bottle of Hennessy, as I do every evening. The decanter and the glass were clean, I assure you. At about two minutes to nine I was coming down the east corridor with a tray when I saw a gentleman in a grey jacket walking away from the study door toward the hall. I couldn't see his face. He was quick on his feet. I didn't think anything of it until later. At 9:43 Mrs. Harrow called me and I phoned the ambulance."

## 12. Witness Statement: Margaret Harrow
[doc_type: witness_statement | person: Margaret Harrow | time: 9:00-9:45 PM]

"I stayed in the drawing room with Oliver, Julian, and the others until Edmund left for his brandy. After that I spoke with several people. I did notice Oliver leave around ten to nine. Julian had gone off to sulk, I assumed, after the quarrel with his father. At twenty to ten I went to ask Edmund to come back for the toast. He was in his chair, utterly still. I touched his hand. It was cold. I screamed for Greaves. I didn't touch the glass or the decanter."

## 13. Witness Statement: Julian Harrow
[doc_type: witness_statement | person: Julian Harrow | time: 8:15-9:45 PM]

"Yes, we argued. About money, as always. He said he was 'finished bailing me out.' I was furious, but I'd never hurt him. I went to the billiard room at nine and played alone until Oliver found me around a quarter to ten. Wait. Oliver found me, yes, maybe a quarter to ten. We were going to rejoin the party when we heard Mother scream." *(Investigator's note: Finch had earlier said he returned to the drawing room at 9:15. See Section 16.)*

## 14. Witness Statement: Dr. Priya Nair and Clara Voss
[doc_type: witness_statement | person: Priya Nair, Clara Voss | time: 9:05-9:40 PM]

Both women gave consistent statements. Dr. Nair: "Clara and I went to the conservatory after dinner. We talked about orchids until Mrs. Harrow's scream." Voss: "That's right. Priya showed me her phalaenopsis photos on her phone. We never left." Margaret Harrow independently confirms seeing them together at 9:10 PM. The conservatory has no door to the east corridor.

## 15. Witness Statement: Oliver Finch
[doc_type: witness_statement | person: Oliver Finch | time: 8:50-9:45 PM]

"I took a call from Zurich in the garden, near the greenhouse, because the reception inside is dreadful. It ran a while. I came back inside at quarter past nine and had a drink with Margaret. Later I went looking for Julian and found him in the billiard room. As for the cufflink, I lost it ages ago. I've no idea how it got under Edmund's desk. Anyone could have picked it up and put it there."

## 16. Evidence E1: The Decanter
[doc_type: evidence | evidence_id: E1 | lab: Ashford Forensic Lab]

The decanter contained brandy laced with a concentrated foxglove extract, with an estimated digitoxin content several times the fatal dose. Only one set of fingerprints beyond Greaves's was found on the stopper: a partial print, too smeared for a match. The seal of the original Hennessy bottle, retrieved from the kitchen bin, was intact when Greaves opened it, which indicates the poison was added to the decanter after 8:30 PM.

## 17. Evidence E2: The Brandy Glass
[doc_type: evidence | evidence_id: E2]

The glass held residue matching the decanter's contents. Only Edmund Harrow's fingerprints were found. The glass was knocked from his hand as he collapsed.

## 18. Evidence E3: The Torn Letter
[doc_type: evidence | evidence_id: E3]

A typed letter on appraisal-firm letterhead was partly burned in the fireplace. Legible fragments include: "...three of the seven paintings in the flagship collection are forgeries...", "...originals appear to have been sold privately between 2019 and 2023...", and "...recommend you notify the insurer immediately." The signature block reads "C. Voss." The letter was dated 10 October. Edmund's desk blotter held a handwritten note: "Confront O. Sunday. Ring Mr. Aldous about the insurer."

## 19. Evidence E4: Phone Records
[doc_type: evidence | evidence_id: E4]

Oliver Finch's mobile records show a single outgoing call to a Zurich number at 8:52 PM that lasted 3 minutes and 10 seconds. No other calls or data sessions occurred between 8:55 and 9:45 PM. His phone's last location ping before 9:15 PM places it near the east wing. Dr. Nair's and Clara Voss's phones show a continuous photo-sharing session with no movement from 9:05 to 9:40 PM.

## 20. Evidence E5: The Cufflink
[doc_type: evidence | evidence_id: E5]

A 9-carat gold cufflink engraved "O.F." was found under Edmund's desk. The housekeeper's log records that the study was thoroughly vacuumed and dusted on Friday 13 October, the day before the party, and that nothing was found under the desk. Finch said he lost the cufflink in September.

## 21. Evidence E6: The Greenhouse
[doc_type: evidence | evidence_id: E6]

Fresh cuttings had been taken from a foxglove plant in the greenhouse. A single muddy size-10 shoeprint was found inside the door. The greenhouse's key is kept on a hook in the kitchen, and the lock showed no signs of tampering. A pair of secateurs, wiped clean, was returned to the potting bench rather than the hook where the gardener always leaves them. The greenhouse sits just outside the garden where Finch said he took his call.

## 22. Evidence E7: Toxicology Report
[doc_type: evidence | evidence_id: E7 | author: Dr. Samuel Okoye, pathologist]

Time of death is estimated between 9:25 and 9:45 PM. Blood digitoxin levels were well over ten times the therapeutic range. The substance is consistent with a plant extract rather than a pharmaceutical preparation; pharmaceutical digoxin or digitoxin would typically show a different impurity profile. This points away from Dr. Nair's medical supplies.

## 23. Investigator's Open Questions
[doc_type: notes | author: Inspector Whitlock]

Whose grey jacket did Greaves see in the corridor at 8:58 PM? Why do Finch and Julian disagree about when they met? How did a cufflink missing since September get under a desk that was cleaned the day before? Why was a call that lasted barely three minutes said to have kept Finch outside for over twenty? What were the "inventory" concerns that Edmund planned to confront someone about on Sunday?
